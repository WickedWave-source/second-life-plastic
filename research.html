CAS stage: Investigation

What we found out

We didn’t want to assume what people knew, so we asked first — then checked what reliable sources say about plastic in Cambodia.

Part A — Asking ISPP

We walked around school with our phones and asked students who agreed to take part a short, anonymous survey.

  • When: [dates of survey]
  • Who: [number] students from Grades [x–y], all voluntary and anonymous
  • How: a Google Form on our phones, about 2 minutes each

What the survey told us

  • [Finding 1 — e.g. most students knew plastic is a problem but not how big it is in Cambodia]
  • [Finding 2 — e.g. few knew what the recycling numbers mean]
  • [Finding 3 — e.g. students prefer photo steps to long text, so we built our guides that way]

Part B — What the facts say

  • Phnom Penh produces 3,000–5,000 tonnes of waste a day, and about 20% of it is plastic. (Phnom Penh Post)
  • About 10 million plastic bags are used in Phnom Penh every day, and each person in the city uses more than 2,000 a year. (Phnom Penh Post)
  • In 2022, only about 5% of Cambodia’s plastic waste was properly managed and less than 2% was recycled, according to the government’s Plastic Action Roadmap with UNDP. (UCA News)
  • Up to 203 tonnes of plastic a day flow from Phnom Penh into the Mekong in the rainy season. (VOD English)
  • Worldwide, only 9% of plastic was recycled in 2019. (OECD Global Plastics Outlook, via Asia News Network)

Part C — Which plastics are safe to reuse?

Not every plastic is safe to cut, reuse or keep near food. Before using anything, we checked the number inside the recycling triangle.

CodeNameCommon itemsSafe for our projects?
1PETWater and soda bottlesYes for crafts and planters; don’t refill with hot drinks
2HDPEMilk jugs, shampoo bottlesYes — our main material; strong and easy to cut
4LDPEPlastic bags, squeeze bottlesYes for weaving and simple crafts
5PPYogurt pots, bottle capsYes for small parts and mosaics
3PVCPipes, some packagingAvoid — can release harmful chemicals
6PSFoam boxes, plastic cutleryAvoid — brittle, crumbles into microplastics
7OtherMixed plasticsAvoid — unknown contents

Safety rule for every project on this site: never burn or melt plastic. The fumes are toxic.
