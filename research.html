The journey: from a Phnom Penh street to the sea

A bag used for five minutes can travel through our drains, canals and rivers all the way to the South China Sea.

  1. 🛍️

    Used once

    A bag, cup or bottle is used for a few minutes.

    10 million bags a day in Phnom Penh [6]
  2. 🗑️

    Thrown away

    It is dropped, dumped or left uncollected.

    Only 54% of Cambodia’s plastic waste is collected [3]
  3. 🌧️

    Washed into drains

    Rain sweeps litter into street drains, where bags block the water.

    Blocked drains make flooding worse [9]
  4. 🏞️

    Canals

    Drains empty into the city’s canals.

    1,500 t of rubbish, mostly plastic, pulled from one canal in 3 weeks [8]
  5. 🌊

    Rivers

    The Tonle Sap, Mekong and Bassac carry it downstream.

    Up to 203 t of plastic a day leave Phnom Penh by river [7]
  6. 🐟

    The sea

    Through the Mekong Delta to the South China Sea, where it breaks into microplastics that fish eat.

Part B · 4 of 6

What harm does it do?

Plastic pollution isn’t just ugly. It affects the air we breathe, floods our streets, gets into our food and heats the planet.

🔥 Air and health

45% burned

Most plastic in Cambodia that isn’t collected is burned. The Ministry of Environment warns the smoke releases dioxins and furans, which can cause breathing problems and even cancer.

Sources [3][10]

🌧️ Flooding

1,500 tonnes

of rubbish, mostly plastic, was cleared from Phnom Penh’s Boeung Trabek canal in March 2021 to stop it flooding. City officials say plastic bags block drains in the rainy season.

Sources [8][9]

🐟 Rivers, fish and food

Into the food chain

Plastic never fully disappears: it breaks into tiny microplastics. A 2025 study found microplastics in the edible flesh of fish caught in the South China Sea — where the Mekong ends.

Sources [11][13]

🌍 Climate

98% fossil fuels

98% of plastic made from new material comes from oil, gas or coal, and burning plastic releases carbon dioxide — so more plastic means more greenhouse gases.

Sources [2][10]

How long does it last?

A plastic bottle thrown away in 2026 could still be in the sea in the year 2476.

Source: NOAA / Woods Hole Sea Grant, via World Economic Forum [12]

Being honest about these numbers: they are estimates, because plastic has only existed in large amounts since the 1960s. Scientists say “450 years” is an illustration, not a measured fact [13]. And “breaking down” doesn’t mean disappearing — plastic splits into smaller and smaller microplastics. Cambodia’s Ministry of Environment gives a range of 10 to 800 years depending on the type [10].

What actually helps?

What’s being done

WhoWhat
CambodiaIn January 2026 the government launched the Plastic Action Roadmap (2026–2040). It aims to raise plastic circularity from 2% to 52% and cut mismanaged plastic by up to 74% by 2040, with ideas like banning avoidable single-use plastics, a deposit-return scheme for bottles and education in schools [3][4].
The worldSince 2022, UN countries have been negotiating a global plastics treaty. Talks in Geneva in August 2025 ended without agreement, and a new chair was elected in February 2026 to restart them [14][15].
UsWe can’t change laws, but we can show the ISPP community that reuse is easy. That’s why this website has DIY guides and a challenge.
Part C

Which plastics are safe to reuse?

Not every plastic is safe to cut, reuse or keep near food. Before using anything, we checked the number inside the recycling triangle.

CodeNameCommon itemsSafe for our projects?
1PETWater and soda bottlesYes for crafts and planters; don’t refill with hot drinks
2HDPEMilk jugs, shampoo bottlesYes — our main material; strong and easy to cut
4LDPEPlastic bags, squeeze bottlesYes for weaving and simple crafts
5PPYogurt pots, bottle capsYes for small parts and mosaics
3PVCPipes, some packagingAvoid — can release harmful chemicals
6PSFoam boxes, plastic cutleryAvoid — brittle, crumbles into microplastics
7OtherMixed plasticsAvoid — unknown contents

Safety rule for every project on this site: never burn or melt plastic. The fumes are toxic [10].
